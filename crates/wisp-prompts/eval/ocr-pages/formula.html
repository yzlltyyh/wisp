Example 2.3. Let a, b, c be real numbers with a ≠ 0. The roots of the quadratic equation ax2 + bx + c = 0 are given by

x = −b ± b2 − 4ac 2a .

The quantity b2 − 4ac is called the discriminant: the equation has two distinct real roots when it is positive. Two further classical results are

∞ Σ n=1 1n2 = π26 and ∫ +∞−∞ e−x2 dx = π.

Both identities are used in Section 4 to bound the error of the numerical scheme by O(h2) for step size h → 0.
