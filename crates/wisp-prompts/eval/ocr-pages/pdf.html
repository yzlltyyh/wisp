3.2Replicated Clipboard History

Modern desktop assistants rarely live on a single machine. A user may copy
a code snippet on a laptop at home and expect to paste it on an office work-
station only a few minutes later. We therefore treat the clipboard history as a
replicated log rather than a local cache: each new entry is encrypted on the
device that has produced it, uploaded to a storage endpoint the user controls,
and then merged by every other device through a deliberately narrow inter-
face that exposes nothing but opaque blobs and version vectors.
This choice was shaped by the conditions under which the first prototype
was evaluated. During the COVID-19 lockdowns most participants worked
from home, where several family members often shared one congested Wi-
Fi connection, and uploads would stall for seconds at a time. To keep the user
interface responsive, synchronization runs in a background task with expo-
nential back-off, while the visible history is updated optimistically; a con-
flict is surfaced only when two devices modify the same entry concurrently,
which in our logs happened in fewer than 0.3% of all recorded sessions.
Section 4 describes the merge procedure in more detail, and Section 5 reports
measurements collected over six weeks on 41 laptops and desktops.
